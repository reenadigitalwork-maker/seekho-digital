PREPARE TO EXPLAIN

Interview Questions

Practise explaining ideas in your own words. Open a question to see the key points a strong beginner answer could cover.

How to practise an answer

Try answering aloud in 60–90 seconds. Use a simple structure: define the idea, give an example, explain why it matters and mention a limitation where useful.

Do not memorise these notes word-for-word. Interviewers are more interested in whether you understand the idea and can apply it.

Beginner interview question bank

1. What is digital marketing?
Include: Marketing through digital channels; understanding an audience; using suitable content and tools; measuring what happens. Example: A customer finds a bakery through search, checks its website and contacts the business.
2. What is the difference between marketing and advertising?
Include: Marketing is the broader process of understanding and serving customers. Advertising is one way to promote an offer. Example: Research, offer design and customer service are marketing activities; a paid social ad is advertising.
3. What is a target audience?
Include: A defined group a business chooses to serve; needs and situations; evidence rather than stereotypes. Example: People planning small celebrations who need easy-to-order dessert portions.
4. What is a customer persona and what are its limits?
Include: A research-informed summary of a typical audience member; needs, motivations and barriers; it is a useful model, not a real individual or a substitute for ongoing research.
5. What makes a piece of content effective?
Include: Clear purpose, audience relevance, accuracy, useful detail, suitable format and a next step. Mention how you would check whether it helped.
6. What is a call to action?
Include: A clear instruction that tells a reader what to do next. Example: “View the weekend menu” for someone exploring an offer.
7. What is a landing page?
Include: A focused page that supports a particular campaign or action; message match; useful details; a clear CTA; mobile usability.
8. What is SEO?
Include: Helping search engines understand useful content and helping people discover relevant pages; search intent; helpful page structure; no guaranteed rankings.
9. What is the difference between a keyword and search intent?
Include: A keyword is the phrase searched; intent is what the person is trying to achieve. A page should respond to that intent, not just repeat the phrase.
10. How would you choose a social media platform?
Include: Audience behaviour, business goal, content format, available time and ability to respond consistently. Explain why you would test rather than assume.
11. What is the difference between organic and paid social?
Include: Organic distribution does not pay for placement but takes time and effort. Paid promotion uses a budget for distribution. Both can happen on the same platform; results vary.
12. Why is permission important in email marketing?
Include: Respecting consent, privacy and expectations; sending relevant messages; identifying the sender; providing an unsubscribe option; following applicable rules.
13. What is an email open rate?
Include: A ratio of recorded opens to a defined email denominator; the provider's formula matters; privacy features can affect open tracking, so it is not perfect proof someone read the email.
14. What factors affect paid advertising results?
Include: Objective, audience, competition, bid or auction, creative, budget, schedule and landing-page experience. Explain that spending more does not guarantee customers.
15. What is A/B testing?
Include: Comparing versions to learn which performs better against a defined metric; change one main variable where possible; use enough data; record test conditions.
16. What is the difference between a metric and a KPI?
Include: A metric is a measurement. A KPI is a key metric tied to an important goal. Context and the business objective decide whether a number is useful.
17. How would you report on a campaign?
Include: Goal, time period, data source, metric definitions, result, insight, limitation and next action. Do not claim causation without evidence.
18. How would you respond if a campaign did not perform well?
Include: Check tracking and objective first; review audience, message, creative and page; identify evidence; propose one test; avoid blaming the audience or changing everything at once.
19. How can a beginner show practical digital marketing skills?
Include: A portfolio with a brief, audience notes, content samples, page outline, channel plan, measurement approach and honest reflection. Clearly label simulated projects.
20. What would you do in your first week on a small-business marketing project?
Include: Ask about business goals, customers, offer, service area and constraints; review existing assets; agree on a measurable first step; avoid promising results before research.

Practise your own answer

Choose a question above and write a short answer in your own words.
